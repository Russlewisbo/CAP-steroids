Sampler diagnostics

Plots of chain mixing, energy, prior against posterior and posterior-predictive fit are on the convergence page. Every fit in the project was run with four chains and accepted only if R-hat was below 1.01, bulk and tail effective sample sizes were above 400 and there were no divergent transitions. The table summarises the 161 fits by group; all pass. The lowest effective sample size among the main fits is 1200.

Simulation checks

Before any real fit, the Stan model was checked on simulated data shaped like F1 (R/01_simulate.R): its prior-predictive distribution matched direct draws from the priors, and parameter recovery was assessed on 40 simulated datasets per scenario with known μ and τ.

Code 
sim_rec |> transmute(`True OR` = round(exp(mu_true), 2), `True tau` = tau_true, Parameter = variable,
                     `Coverage of 95% CrI` = coverage95, `Bias (median)` = round(bias_median, 3),
                     `Max R-hat` = round(max_rhat, 3), `Divergences (total over 40 fits)` = total_divergences) |>
  kable(caption = "Recovery on simulated data (1000 post-warmup draws, adapt_delta 0.95; the real fits use 2000 draws and 0.99).")

Some simulated fits had a few divergences at adapt_delta 0.95, so the real fits were run at 0.99; none of the real fits has divergences. With only six or seven trials, τ is estimated loosely and tends to be pulled toward its prior. In the simulations the pooled effect μ was recovered with coverage close to the nominal 95% (0.950, 0.975, 1.000 across the three scenarios) and small bias, so the model can recover a known effect from data of this size and shape; it does not show that the real data are well described by the model.

Cross-check against brms

To confirm that the Stan hybrid model reproduces standard software when one likelihood is switched off, each case was refitted in brms (cmdstanr backend, same priors, 4 chains with 2000 post-warmup draws, adapt_delta 0.99):

  • Pure-count: F1 and F4 with the REMAP-CAP contrast removed, events | trials(n) ~ 0 + trial + trt + (0 + trt | trial) (unpooled trial baselines; trt is the pooled log-OR and the random-slope SD is τ).
  • Pure-contrast: the S-iii rows for F1 and F4 (every trial as log-OR and SE; Hagman, a double-zero trial, is dropped in F4), yi | se(sei) ~ 1 + (1 | trial).

Agreement was judged against a criterion fixed before looking at the results, in units of the pooled posterior SD of each quantity: means and medians within 0.10 SD and 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles within 0.20 SD (Monte Carlo error alone is of the order 0.02 to 0.05 SD, larger in the tails). It was assessed for μ, τ and every shrunken trial effect θ.

Frequentist cross-check (REML, Hartung–Knapp)

The protocol promised a frequentist random-effects analysis alongside the Bayesian one, for readers used to that framework. It uses the same rows as the Bayesian F1 and F4 fits. The main method is REML with the Hartung–Knapp adjustment; plain Wald REML is shown alongside. Count trials enter as log-ORs, with 0.5 added to every cell of a trial with a zero cell (Confalonieri), and double-zero trials are dropped (Hagman, F4). REMAP-CAP enters through its ICU-discharge contrast (D2a).

Code 
freq <- tar_read(freq_tab)
freq |>
  transmute(Set = set, Method = method, Trials = k,
            `OR (95% CI)` = or_ci(OR, OR_lo, OR_hi), `95% prediction interval` = or_ci(OR, PI_lo, PI_hi),
            τ = pr(tau), `I² (%)` = sprintf("%.0f", I2), P = sprintf("%.3f", p_value)) |>
  mutate(`95% prediction interval` = sub("^[0-9.]+ ", "", `95% prediction interval`)) |>
  kable(caption = "Frequentist random-effects meta-analysis of the F1 and F4 rows (metafor). For comparison only; the Bayesian fits are the analysis.")
fq <- \(st, m = "REML, Hartung-Knapp") filter(freq, set == st, method == m)

With Hartung–Knapp, the F1 estimate is 0.71 (0.40–1.23), against the Bayesian 0.67 (0.39–1.06), and F4 is 0.75 (0.59–0.96) against 0.73 (0.55–0.93). The point estimates are close. Differences in the intervals come from the small number of trials and from the approximations the frequentist analysis needs: a normal likelihood with a continuity correction for Confalonieri and a single heterogeneity estimate that ignores its uncertainty, which Hartung–Knapp only partly corrects.

Leave-one-trial-out and pooled-baseline comparison

PSIS-LOO is not reliable for this model: for F1, Pareto k exceeds 0.7 for 14 of 18 arm-level and trial-level terms, because each unpooled baseline is identified only by its own trial. Exact leave-one-trial-out refits were used instead, scoring the held-out treatment arm given its control arm (the contrast for REMAP-CAP), for both the primary unpooled-baseline model and a pooled-baseline comparison model.

Code 
loto_cmp$by_trial |>
  left_join(distinct(mortality, trial_id, study_label), by = c("trial" = "trial_id")) |>
  transmute(`Held-out trial` = study_label, `Unpooled baselines` = round(unpooled, 2), `Pooled baselines` = round(pooled, 2),
            Difference = round(diff_pooled_minus_unpooled, 2)) |>
  kable(caption = "Held-out predictive log density (higher is better).")

Total over the seven trials: unpooled -22.4, pooled -21.9, difference 0.54 (SE 0.29). The difference is less than two standard errors and the trial-level differences are small and mixed in sign, so there is no evidence that pooling the baselines improves prediction; the primary unpooled baselines are retained.

The figure below shows how the pooled OR changes when each trial is left out in turn. The pooled median OR ranges from 0.58 (omitting Meduri 2022 (ESCAPe)) to 0.73 (omitting Confalonieri 2005), against 0.67 with all seven trials; P(OR<1) ranges from 0.91 to 0.98. No single trial reverses the direction of the estimate, and the two baseline models give almost the same answers. Leaving out Confalonieri 2005 or Dequin 2023 (CAPE COD) moves the estimate furthest toward 1, and leaving out Meduri 2022 (ESCAPe) or Heming 2025 (REMAP-CAP) moves it furthest away from 1.
