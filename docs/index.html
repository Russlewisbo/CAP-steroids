Draft analysis of adjunctive systemic corticosteroids versus placebo or no corticosteroid in adults with severe community-acquired pneumonia (sCAP): a Bayesian random-effects meta-analysis of all-cause mortality on the odds-ratio scale, with an agent-level hierarchy, a prior ladder and tipping-point analyses. The review is not yet registered. The four main analysis decisions (D1 to D4) have been taken by the project lead; a few further analysis choices listed below still await confirmation.

Abstract

Background. Randomised evidence on adjunctive systemic corticosteroids in severe community-acquired pneumonia (sCAP) is conflicting: hydrocortisone reduced 28-day mortality in CAPE COD, whereas ESCAPe (prolonged methylprednisolone) and REMAP-CAP (hydrocortisone, adaptive platform) did not show a benefit.

Methods. Bayesian random-effects meta-analysis of short-term all-cause mortality on the odds-ratio scale. Trials reporting arm counts enter through an exact binomial likelihood with unpooled trial baselines; REMAP-CAP, whose crude counts are not valid for pooling, enters as an adjusted contrast: its ICU-discharge contrast in the short-term analyses (decision D2a) and its day-90 contrast in the 90-day analysis. The primary set (F1) has seven severe-CAP trials; broader sets cover 90-day mortality (F2), published severe subgroups (F3) and all hospitalised CAP (F4). We also fitted an agent-level hierarchy, a ladder of priors from enthusiastic to harm-leaning with tipping-point analyses, sensitivity fits and meta-regressions, and checked the model on simulated data and against brms. The four analysis decisions (D1 to D4) were taken by the project lead before the final run; their alternatives are reported as sensitivity analyses.

Results. In F1 the pooled odds ratio for death was 0.67 (0.39–1.06) (posterior median and 95% credible interval, reference prior). The posterior probability of any benefit (OR<1) was 0.96 and of an odds reduction of about 17% or more (OR<0.833) was 0.85. At the pooled control risk of 19.8% the absolute risk reduction was 5.7 percentage points (95% credible interval -1.0 to 10.9). Estimates were similar in the broader sets (F3: 0.73 (0.50–1.01); F4: 0.73 (0.55–0.93)). Between-trial heterogeneity was poorly identified (median τ 0.40), and the predictive odds ratio for a new trial was 0.67 (0.20–2.05). Across 14 sensitivity fits the pooled OR ranged from 0.61 to 0.71 and P(OR<1) from 0.85 to 1.00; excluding REMAP-CAP gave the lowest pooled OR, and a common-effect model without between-trial heterogeneity gave the highest probability of benefit (P(OR<1) 1.00). With a skeptical prior P(OR<1) was 0.94; with the two harm-leaning priors the posterior median OR was 0.87 and 0.83 and P(OR<1) was 0.79 and 0.79. The agent hierarchy could not separate hydrocortisone (OR 0.63) from methylprednisolone (OR 0.74; probability that hydrocortisone is more favourable 0.72), and the meta-regressions were uninformative.

Conclusions. The data point towards lower short-term mortality with corticosteroids in sCAP, but not conclusively: the credible interval for the primary estimate includes no effect, and the estimate depends on how REMAP-CAP is handled and on the prior. The second assessor’s confirmation of the risk-of-bias consensus and the investigator-supplied REMAP-CAP estimates are still outstanding. These results are provisional.

TipPlain-language summary

The question. Do steroid medicines, such as hydrocortisone, given alongside antibiotics help adults with severe pneumonia caught outside hospital to survive?

What we did. We combined the results of the randomised trials that tested this in people with severe pneumonia. In these trials, patients were assigned by chance to receive a steroid or not, which makes the groups comparable. Seven trials form the main analysis.

What we found. In the comparison groups about 20 in 100 patients died. Our best estimate is that steroids lowered this by about 6 deaths per 100 patients treated, but this is uncertain: the plausible range runs from about 1.0 more deaths to about 10.9 fewer deaths per 100 patients treated. The analysis puts the chance that steroids lower the risk of dying at about 96 in 100.

Why it is not settled. The trials do not agree. Two of the larger ones (CAPE COD and APROCCHSS) found fewer deaths with hydrocortisone, while another (ESCAPe) and the large platform trial REMAP-CAP did not. The answer also depends on how REMAP-CAP is counted and on how skeptical one is to begin with: starting from a prior belief that steroids might do harm, the chance of benefit falls to about 79 in 100. We cannot tell which steroid works best.

What this means. This is a draft analysis, not a treatment recommendation. The checks on trial quality, some further trials still being looked for, and one key number from REMAP-CAP are still to be settled, so these results may change.

  • D1 (ESCAPe short-term row) — decided: in-hospital. This follows the protocol rule (field 24: earliest of in-hospital, 28-day, 30-day). The trial’s own primary endpoint, day 60, lies outside that window, and the 30-day IPD row is a rank-2 source. The in-hospital row is the least favourable of the three; the trial does not define the length of the in-hospital window. The pooled result moves from P(OR<1) = 0.96 (in-hospital) to 0.98 (d60) and 0.98 (IPD 30-day).
  • D2 (REMAP-CAP in the short-term fit) — decided: D2a. REMAP-CAP enters F1 through its adjusted ICU-discharge contrast, the earliest adjusted mortality contrast it reports; the same contrast is used in the broader short-term sets F3 and F4 (decided 10 November), and its day-90 pooled contrast only in the 90-day analysis (F2). The former default (day-90 contrast in F1) gives 0.68 (0.38–1.09), P(OR<1) = 0.95, and excluding REMAP-CAP (D2b) gives P(OR<1) = 0.98. Both contrast SEs are interim values derived from the published credible intervals (0.487 for ICU discharge, 0.362 for day 90). Varying the ICU-discharge SE from 0.40 to 0.60 gives P(OR<1) 0.96 to 0.97; the corresponding range for the day-90 contrast (D2c, SE 0.30 to 0.45) is 0.94 to 0.96.
  • D3 (prior ladder) — decided as proposed. Enthusiastic N(log 0.70, 0.20), reference N(0, 1.0) and skeptical N(0, 0.35); the mild-harm N(log 1.10, 0.20) and REMAP-CAP-harm N(log 1.50, 0.35) rungs are fixed by the protocol. The reference rung gives the headline result. The enthusiastic rung is built from earlier syntheses that include some of the same trials, so it shows what a believer in those syntheses would conclude rather than adding independent evidence.
  • D4 (τ and ω hyperpriors) — decided as proposed. τ ~ half-Normal(0, 0.5) and ω ~ half-Normal(0, 0.25), with τ half-Normal(0, 0.25) and (0, 1) and ω half-Normal(0, 0.5) as sensitivity fits. The data do not inform ω (its posterior stays close to its prior), so the agent comparison is driven by the prior by design.

Other analysis choices that still need confirmation

  • Meta-regression coefficient prior b ~ Normal(0, 1), applied to covariates centred and (for continuous ones) scaled to SD 1.
  • Pooled-baseline comparison model: a0 ~ Normal(0, 2), sigma_a ~ half-Normal(0, 1) (comparison only; primary baselines stay unpooled).
  • Coding of binary timing_le24h: 1 if the stated maximum window to first dose is ≤24 h, 0 if the stated maximum or median is longer; coding table in the meta-regression section. Torres (within 36 h) and SONIA (within 48 h) are coded 0.
  • S-IPD: the analysis plan lists Snijders and Santeon among F4 IPD replacements, but analysis_mortality.csv has no IPD row for either. The Smit 2025 trial table gives the same 30-day counts as our rank-1 rows for both (6/104 vs 6/109 and 4/203 vs 7/198), so nothing is lost; the trials swapped are Confalonieri, Torres, ESCAPe and CAPE COD in F1, with Meijvis and STEP added in F4.
