Every F1 fit on this page uses decision D2a (REMAP-CAP through its ICU-discharge contrast) unless it changes the REMAP-CAP row itself. Across the 14 F1 sensitivity fits the posterior median OR ranges from 0.61 to 0.71 and P(OR<1) from 0.85 to 1.00, against 0.67 and 0.96 for the primary fit.

What moves the estimate

  • Handling of REMAP-CAP is the largest single influence on the median. Excluding it gives an OR of 0.61 (0.33–0.94) with P(OR<1) = 0.98, the lowest median OR of all F1 fits. Using the day-90 contrast (the former default) instead of the ICU-discharge contrast chosen under D2a changes little (0.68 (0.38–1.09), P(OR<1) = 0.95), and the assumed SE of the day-90 contrast shifts P(OR<1) between 0.94 (SE 0.30) and 0.96 (SE 0.45). The ICU-discharge SE used in the primary fit (0.487) is also derived from the credible interval, which is asymmetric on the log scale (its lower and upper halves imply SEs of 0.45 and 0.53); setting it to 0.40 or 0.60 gives 0.67 (0.38–1.07) and 0.65 (0.39–1.03), with P(OR<1) = 0.96 and 0.97.
  • Heterogeneity prior. A tighter τ prior narrows the interval (0.67 (0.46–0.97)), a wider one widens it (0.65 (0.34–1.15)), with the median essentially unchanged. The interval therefore depends on a prior that the seven trials cannot pin down.
  • Agent model. Letting the two agent clusters differ widely (ω prior SD 0.5) lowers P(OR<1) to 0.85 and widens the interval to 0.71 (0.36–1.56), the least favourable of the F1 fits, because uncertainty about agent effects feeds into the pooled estimate.
  • Dropping single trials by design. Removing APROCCHSS (subgroup-derived and the only mineralocorticoid trial; S-vi and S-viii are identical in F1) gives 0.68 (0.34–1.15), and removing the single-bolus Marik trial gives 0.68 (0.40–1.10). P(OR<1) is 0.93 and 0.95 respectively, against 0.96 for the primary fit: a modest loss of certainty, with no change in direction.
  • Baseline prior and IPD rows. A tighter baseline prior (0.65 (0.38–1.05)) and replacing four trials’ counts with the Smit 2025 IPD rows (0.64 (0.39–0.99)) leave the result essentially unchanged.
  • Likelihood. The contrast-based fit S-iii, which replaces the exact binomial likelihood with a log-OR and SE, gives 0.71 (0.44–1.10), a higher median than the primary fit. It is the only fit that does not use the exact binomial likelihood, so the gap indicates how much the normal approximation and the continuity correction for Confalonieri’s zero cell matter with trials this small.
  • Common-effect model. Fixing τ near zero (no between-trial heterogeneity) while keeping the exact likelihood gives 0.67 (0.53–0.85) in F1 with P(OR<1) = 0.999. The median barely moves, but the interval narrows sharply and the probability of benefit rises to nearly 1, because the model treats the disagreement between trials as pure sampling noise. This is the assumption behind the much higher probabilities in Pitre’s Bayesian analysis (see the benchmark page); we regard the random-effects result as the appropriate primary analysis because the trials differ in design, dose, agent and population.
  • Larger sets. Dropping the subgroup-derived trials from F4 gives 0.74 (0.50–0.99) and using IPD rows in F4 gives 0.73 (0.55–0.90), both close to the F4 result (0.73 (0.55–0.93)); the common-effect F4 fit gives 0.76 (0.66–0.88), and using the IPD placebo count for Meijvis (S-src, below) gives 0.74 (0.56–0.95).

Taken together, the direction of the estimate is stable (every F1 sensitivity fit has a median OR below 1), but the strength of the evidence varies: P(OR<1) falls to 0.85 in the least favourable fit.

Low risk of bias only (provisional)

RoB 2 for all-cause mortality has been completed independently by two assessors for the seven primary-set trials: assessor 1 (an AI draft, to be reviewed) and assessor 2 (a human second assessor). They agree on the overall rating for 5 of 7 trials. Assessor 1 rates 3 of the seven trials low risk of bias and assessor 2 rates 5 low; the trials rated differently are Confalonieri and APROCCHSS (see the risk of bias page for every rating and the consensus rationale). A consensus was recorded on 4 November on the lead’s decision, with the second assessor’s confirmation pending: APROCCHSS low (the open-access design paper lists the CAP subgroup as a priori defined, stratification by centre only, adequate concealment, and an analysis plan written while the trial was still blinded) and Confalonieri some concerns (no registration or protocol, the same rule as for Marik). The analysis below restricts F1 to the low-risk trials for each assessor and for the consensus, and stays provisional until the second assessor confirms. REMAP-CAP is “some concerns” for both and Marik is “high” for both, so neither enters either fit.

With assessor 1’s three low-risk trials (CAPE COD, ESCAPe, Torres) the pooled OR is 0.74 (0.40–1.40) and with assessor 2’s five (adding Confalonieri and APROCCHSS) it is 0.62 (0.34–1.03), against 0.67 (0.39–1.06) for all seven. The consensus set (CAPE COD, ESCAPe, Torres and APROCCHSS) gives 0.69 (0.43–1.15). The assessors’ sets differ because Confalonieri and APROCCHSS, both of which favour corticosteroids (Confalonieri most strongly of any primary-set trial), are inside one set and outside the other; the consensus keeps APROCCHSS in and Confalonieri out, which gives an estimate between the two assessors’ results. With only three to five trials the intervals are wide in every fit.

Notes on individual analyses

  • S-ii (low risk of bias only) restricts F1 to the trials rated low risk of bias for all-cause mortality. It is shown below for each assessor and for the consensus.
  • S-iii converts each count trial to a log-OR and SE, adding 0.5 to all four cells of any trial with a zero cell (Confalonieri) and dropping double-zero trials. It is the only fit that uses a continuity correction; the forest plot on the results page also adds 0.5 to Confalonieri’s raw estimate, for display only.
  • S-CE is a common-effect sensitivity fit: the same model with the between-trial SD forced near zero through a very tight half-Normal(0, 0.001) prior, run in F1, F3 and F4.
  • S-src replaces the Meijvis placebo 30-day death count of 11 in the primary report (Table 2) by the 9 given by two analyses of the trial’s patient-level data (Wittermans 2022, Table 6, and Smit 2025, Table 1). Every other Meijvis outcome agrees across the three sources (dexamethasone arm 9 of 151 at 30 days, in-hospital deaths 8 and 8, ICU admissions 7 and 10), so the disagreement is confined to two post-discharge deaths in the placebo arm. The documents available here cannot say whether it reflects a different 30-day window, a corrected dataset or an error. The F4 pooled OR moves from 0.73 (0.55–0.93) to 0.74 (0.56–0.95) (P(OR<1) 0.992 to 0.991), which does not affect any conclusion; the primary-report count stays in the main fits, following the source hierarchy.
  • S-iv varies the τ prior (half-Normal SD 0.25 and 1) and, in the agent model, the ω prior (SD 0.5).
  • S-v uses a tighter baseline prior, Normal(0, 2), instead of Normal(0, 10).
  • S-vi, S-viii and S-ix drop the subgroup-derived trial (APROCCHSS; in F4 also Tongyoo), the mineralocorticoid trial (APROCCHSS) and the single-bolus short-course trial (Marik).
  • S-IPD replaces rank-1 rows with Smit 2025 IPD rank-2 rows (cross-check only; never merged with the rank-1 row for the same trial).
