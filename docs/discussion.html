This is a provisional discussion of draft results. It uses the pre-specified defaults for the four open analysis decisions (D1 to D4, see the home page) and will need revising when those decisions, the risk-of-bias assessment and the REMAP-CAP standard error are settled.

Principal findings

In the primary analysis of seven severe-CAP trials, corticosteroids were associated with lower short-term mortality: pooled OR 0.68 (0.38–1.10), with a posterior probability of any benefit of 0.95. The estimate is probable rather than established. The credible interval includes 1, the median absolute risk reduction of 5.4 percentage points has an interval from -1.5 to 11.2 points, and the predictive interval for a new trial (0.68 (0.18–2.28)) comfortably includes harm. The broader sets point the same way (F3 0.74 (0.49–1.05); F4 0.74 (0.55–0.95)), with the F4 interval excluding 1, but F4 answers a wider question and mixes severe and non-severe populations.

The uncertainty largely reflects disagreement between the trials. Raw odds ratios are below 1 for Marik 1993, Confalonieri 2005, Torres 2015, Dequin 2023 (CAPE COD), Heming 2024 (APROCCHSS) and at or above 1 for Meduri 2022 (ESCAPe), Heming 2025 (REMAP-CAP). This pattern matches the published narrative of a benefit in CAPE COD, no benefit in ESCAPe and a harm-leaning result in REMAP-CAP. A random-effects model cannot say why the trials differ, only how much they do; here the between-trial SD is moderate (median 0.47) but poorly identified, because there are only seven trials.

The role of REMAP-CAP

REMAP-CAP is, together with ESCAPe, the most influential trial in the analysis: leaving out either lowers the pooled median OR to about 0.60 (REMAP-CAP) or 0.60 (ESCAPe), and how REMAP-CAP is handled is a judgement call. Its crude counts cannot be pooled (response-adaptive randomisation, non-equipoise sites, control crossover), so its adjusted day-90 contrast enters F1 even though F1 is a short-term analysis, and its standard error is still an interim value derived from the published credible interval. Excluding REMAP-CAP gives 0.60 (0.33–0.97), with P(OR<1) = 0.98; varying the contrast’s SE from 0.30 to 0.45 moves P(OR<1) between 0.94 and 0.96. The investigator-supplied SE and the choice of REMAP-CAP timepoint (D2) will therefore matter for the final result. The question of what REMAP-CAP should contribute to a short-term estimate is a design question for the review, not something the data can settle.

Agent, dose and trial characteristics

The agent hierarchy was intended to ask whether hydrocortisone differs from other agents without the pitfalls of subgrouping. It cannot answer that here. Hydrocortisone has the lower point estimate (0.66 against 0.75 for methylprednisolone) but the probability that it is truly the more favourable agent is only 0.68, the between-agent SD stays at its prior, and each trial used a single agent, so agent is confounded with every other trial characteristic. The meta-regressions are no more informative: 22 were fitted, only 1 has an interval excluding zero (the F1 dose coefficient, which rests on five trials spanning 50 to 60 mg/day prednisone-equivalent and is not a dose-response estimate), and about that many would be expected by chance.

Sensitivity to the prior and to modelling choices

The conclusion is moderately robust to the prior. A skeptical prior reduces P(OR<1) only to 0.92, and on the tipping-point analysis P(OR<1) stays above 0.90 for skeptical priors with an SD of 0.30 or wider. Harm-leaning priors pull the posterior median to 0.89 and 0.86, still below 1, with a probability of benefit of about three in four; only a prior that is both centred on harm and held confidently reverses the direction. The 90-day set is more fragile: under the same harm-leaning priors its median OR is close to 1 (0.96 and 0.98), reflecting three trials and the weight of REMAP-CAP. Across the F1 sensitivity analyses the median OR never exceeds 0.74.

Comparison with other syntheses

The closest published synthesis is Pitre et al. 2025 (29 trials with short-term mortality, RR 0.82 (0.74–0.91)). Converted to an odds ratio at their stated control risk, their estimate (0.79 (0.70–0.89)) is close to our F4 result and to the F3 and F1 intervals, which are wider and extend above 1. Pitre report a probability of 0.87 that the OR is below 0.9; ours is 0.89 for F1 and 0.94 for F4. The two analyses differ in trial set, effect scale, time horizon and priors, and Pitre do not report a severe-only mortality estimate in the main text, so no like-for-like comparison for severe CAP is possible. We have not yet compared our results with the Briel 2018 individual-patient-data analysis.

Strengths

  • The likelihood is exact for sparse count data (no continuity corrections in the primary fit) and a contrast likelihood is used for the one trial whose valid estimate is an adjusted contrast.
  • Trial baselines are unpooled, and a pooled-baseline comparison model gives a total held-out predictive log density of -22.2 against -22.7 for the primary model (difference 0.5, SE 0.3), so this choice does not appear to drive the results.
  • The model was validated on simulated data and reproduced by an independent implementation in brms (53 of 53 compared parameters within a pre-stated tolerance).
  • Prior influence is reported explicitly (ladder, one- and two-dimensional tipping points) rather than through a single analysis, and decisions that belong to the project lead are shown with their alternatives.
  • The whole analysis is reproducible from a single pipeline, so a corrected data row triggers only the affected fits.

Limitations

  • Few trials, heterogeneous contributions. The primary set has seven trials, one contrast-based and one subgroup-derived (APROCCHSS, a shock-only population, 96% ventilated). Its short-term outcome mixes ICU mortality (Marik), in-hospital mortality (Confalonieri, Torres, ESCAPe), day-28 mortality (CAPE COD, APROCCHSS) and, for REMAP-CAP, day-90 mortality. This is by rule, but the timepoints are not identical.
  • Open inputs. Risk of bias has not been assessed (19 of 19 trials pending, so the low-risk-of-bias sensitivity analysis could not be run), the REMAP-CAP SE is interim, screening was a single AI-assisted pass, extraction has had a numeric second check but not a row-by-row sign-off, and one pre-specified IPD replacement could not be done for two trials that have no IPD row in the dataset.
  • Aggregate data. The analysis uses published aggregate data, so effect modification by patient characteristics cannot be examined, and the meta-regressions are across-trial (ecological) analyses.
  • Possible missing trials. Some registered trials are unpublished or ongoing, and publication bias cannot be assessed formally with this few trials.
  • Heterogeneity and agent priors. Results depend on half-Normal priors for τ and ω that the data cannot fully inform; the sensitivity analysis shows the interval but not the median is affected.
  • Draft status. The report is AI-assisted, has not been peer reviewed, and the review is not yet registered.

Implications and next steps

These results do not by themselves support a clinical recommendation. They do suggest which pieces of information would matter most for a final analysis: the REMAP-CAP adjusted effect and its standard error, the day-28 or in-hospital definition for ESCAPe (D1), the prior ladder (D3), and the risk-of-bias judgements. A larger body of severe-CAP trials, particularly any that report a pre-specified 28-day or in-hospital mortality and a common set of timepoints, would reduce the dependence on τ and the prior. Because the pipeline is parameterised by the data file, each of these changes is a re-run rather than a rebuild.

Conclusion

Under the pre-specified defaults, the available randomised evidence leans towards lower short-term mortality with corticosteroids in severe community-acquired pneumonia (OR about 0.68, probability of any benefit 0.95). That conclusion is not secure: the primary interval includes no effect, it depends on how the adaptive REMAP-CAP trial is handled and on the prior, the agent question is unresolved, and several inputs are outstanding. The estimate should be treated as provisional.
